Lesson 1

What Is Trigonometry?

Trigonometry studies the relationship between angles and the sides of triangles. An angle (measured in degrees, °) tells how much something turns: 90° is a right angle, 360° is a full circle.

In a right-angled triangle, for the angle θ: the hypotenuse is the longest side, the opposite side faces θ, and the adjacent side touches θ.

sin θ = Opposite / Hypotenuse
cos θ = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
tan θ = Opposite / Adjacent

Lesson 2

Understanding Coordinates

The Cartesian coordinate system places every point using two numbers. The x-axis is horizontal (right is positive), the y-axis is vertical (up is positive), and they cross at the origin (0, 0). Left of the origin x is negative; below it y is negative.

A game character's position is stored as (x, y). Moving right adds to x; moving up adds to y. Drag the point on the grid!

Note: screens draw y downward, but we use the standard maths system where y points up.

Lesson 3

Sine and Cosine in Character Movement

A game engine knows a direction angle θ and a distance d. It needs to know how far to move sideways (x) and up (y). The movement forms the hypotenuse of a right triangle, so:

x = d × cos(θ) y = d × sin(θ)

x = horizontal movement · y = vertical movement · d = total distance · θ = direction angle.

Worked example: d = 10 units, θ = 30°

x = 10 × cos(30°) ≈ 8.66


y = 10 × sin(30°) = 5

The character moves about 8.66 units horizontally and 5 units vertically.

Maths uses degrees; JavaScript wants radians, so we convert first: radians = degrees × π / 180.

In a game, the player presses a direction (say 30° up-right) and the engine multiplies the speed or distance by cos θ and sin θ to get the sideways and upward steps. Each frame, those steps are added to the character's position. The sign of cos and sin automatically gives negative movement in the left and down directions, so all four quadrants work with the same two formulas.

Where Is Trigonometry Used in Games?

⚠️ These are original, illustrative drawings made for this project — not official screenshots. Real games combine trigonometry with vectors, matrices, geometry, physics and much more.

Example A · Minecraft-style

Block worlds use (x, y, z) coordinates. When a player turns to face a direction, the game uses an angle (yaw) and cos/sin to find which way "forward" points on the ground, then moves along it. Rotations and direction vectors keep orientation consistent.

Example B · Fortnite-style

To aim at a target, the game subtracts positions: dx = targetX − playerX, dy = targetY − playerY. The aim angle is found with θ = atan2(dy, dx) (inverse tangent), and the distance with the Pythagoras theorem. Sine and cosine then turn that angle back into a direction for the shot.

Example C · Racing game

A car stores a heading angle. Steering changes θ a little every frame, and the car advances by (cos θ, sin θ) × speed. Rotating the car's drawing around its centre uses sine and cosine too — a coordinate transformation.

Calculus in Games: Integration

Concept

Adding up tiny changes

An integral adds up many tiny pieces. In a physics engine, velocity tells how fast position changes and acceleration tells how fast velocity changes. To find the new position, the engine integrates:

v(t) = ∫ a dt x(t) = ∫ v dt

For constant acceleration a from rest: v = a·t distance = ∫₀ᵀ a·t dt = ½·a·T²

Computers cannot solve calculus exactly every frame, so games use numerical integration: each frame (a small time step dt) they run x += v·dt; v += a·dt. The integral is the area under the velocity–time graph, and every frame adds one thin bar of that area. More frames per second means thinner bars and a more accurate answer.

Integration appears in vehicle physics, gravity and jumping, projectiles, ragdolls, smoke and water simulation, and in rendering, where light is summed over areas and angles.

Coordinate Geometry: Hitboxes & Aim

Concept

Shapes and lines on the grid

A hitbox is an invisible shape around a player or enemy, defined by coordinates. For a rectangle: x from xmin to xmax and y from ymin to ymax. A point is inside when:

xmin ≤ x ≤ xmax and ymin ≤ y ≤ ymax

Circle hitboxes use the distance formula:

d = √((x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²)

A shot is a line from the shooter: y = m·x + c with slope m = tan θ. The angle needed to aim at a target is θ = atan2(dy, dx). If the line passes through the hitbox, it is a hit.

College Microproject

 

  
Aim
To study and demonstrate the applications of trigonometry in video game development.

   
Objectives
  • Understand basic trigonometric ratios.
  • Learn how coordinates represent game positions.
  • Demonstrate how sine and cosine calculate movement.
  • Explore the relationship between mathematics and computer graphics.
  • Understand how integration (calculus) accumulates motion in physics engines.
  • Apply coordinate geometry to hitboxes and aiming.

   
Materials Required
  • Computer or laptop.
  • Web browser.
  • HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.
  • Interactive simulator.

  
Procedure
  1. Open the movement simulator.
  2. Select a direction angle.
  3. Select a movement distance.
  4. Observe the movement vector.
  5. Record the horizontal and vertical components.
  6. Compare the results with manual mathematical calculations.

   
Conclusion
Trigonometry provides mathematical tools for calculating direction, position, rotation, and movement in game development.
